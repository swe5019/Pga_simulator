Hi,

The NFL player props tool is up on SlateSims and free to use. It covers every
line Underdog posts, which is about five thousand this week. It is there to be
tested rather than because it is finished, so I want to hear what is wrong
with it.

Three numbers sit beside every line.

**BE%** is break even, straight from the price. It is how often a pick has to
win before it stops losing you money.

**Hit%** is how often that player has actually cleared that number, with games
where he had a different role counted for less.

**SIM%** rebuilds the game from his individual plays and runs it thousands of
times. It never looks at the price, so it is free to disagree with the market.
When Hit% and SIM% both clear the price, that is a much stronger signal than
either on its own, and there is a filter for exactly that.

There is also a matchup figure for what the defense he is facing has allowed
his position in that stat. Hover it and you get the week by week reasoning
behind it.

## One warning, and please read this one

Be careful with any player stepping into a bigger role because somebody ahead
of him is hurt.

Everything in the tool is built from games already played. When a starter is
ruled out, the book reprices his backup within minutes. The tool cannot do that
on its own, because the backup has no history at the bigger job. So it compares
a backup's old workload against a line set for a starter and reports an
enormous edge, almost always on the under. Those are the biggest edges on the
board and they are worthless.

I have built two guards for it, and you should still check the news yourself.

A **triangle** beside a name means somebody ahead of him is out, and his role
is being read from the games that man missed rather than from his season
average. Hover the name and it tells you who is out, whether he is ruled out or
simply has not practised, and what this player's role was the last time that
man sat.

A **cross** means the player is on the injury report himself.

A **warning sign** means the tool and the market disagree sharply about how
much work he is going to get. On those rows the edge figures are greyed out and
kept out of the filters. Treat it as a reminder to go and read the injury news,
not as a number to bet.

If a player has never played a game without the man who is out, the tool says
it cannot judge him and leans on the line instead. I would rather it admit that
than invent a projection for you.

## What it is good at, and what it is bad at

I tested it by running it forward through two seasons it was never fitted on,
roughly one hundred and forty thousand predictions. Picks it called at 74.7%
came in at 74.7%, and no confidence band is off by more than a single point.

It is strongest on longest reception, longest rush and longest completion.
Counting past games tells you almost nothing about whether a receiver breaks
one, and simulating the individual plays tells you a lot.

It is weakest, close to useless, on quarterback passing attempts and passing
yards. How often a quarterback throws is decided mostly by the score, and the
tool has no idea what the score will be. I would ignore those two markets for
now, and the page says so too.
