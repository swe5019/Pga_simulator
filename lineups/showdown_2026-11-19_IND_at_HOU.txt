WHAT CHANGED FROM THE FIRST VERSION, AND WHY
==============================================================================

A flex ownership model now exists, and it overturned the earlier lineup 3.

That build looked like the contrarian one because its captain was owned 0.5%.
Priced across all six slots it came to 198.3% of field share, ABOVE all five
scored winners and above the 90th percentile of the winners this simulation
found for tonight. The captain was lonely and the other five slots were the
most crowded in the game: Tyler Warren is modelled at 43.5% of the field while
appearing in only 28.7% of winning lineups, the second largest such gap on the
board. Warren is gone. The rebuilt dart sits at 163.1%.

This is the whole case for modelling the flex slot. A captain-only view said
that lineup was different from the field. It was not.

THE CAPTAIN QUESTION, RESOLVED RATHER THAN ASSERTED

The simulation prefers C.J. Stroud as captain and the five scored contests
have never once been won by a captained quarterback. Both are true, and they
agree once the target score is named. Searching all 220,932 legal lineups for
the one most likely to clear a given score:

    clear 140 points    Stroud captain wins, 10.82% against Collins 10.38%
    clear 160 points    Stroud captain wins, 4.17% against Collins 4.10%
    clear 180 points    COLLINS wins, 1.43% against Stroud 1.38%
    clear 200 points    COLLINS wins, 0.60% against Stroud 0.55%

The crossover is near 170. Below it you want the quarterback's floor; above it
you want a receiver's tail. A 643,587-entry tournament is won well above that
line, which is why none of these three captains a quarterback, and why the
record of five contests is not a coincidence. If you were playing a small
field or a double-up, Stroud captain is the better build and lineup 1 is not.

WHAT THE OWNERSHIP BUDGET DID NOT DO

It barely bound. The highest-ceiling lineups on this slate already sit at 140%
to 155% of field share, comfortably under every scored winner. The reason is
that the field's overpriced players are ones you did not want anyway: Davis
Mills is modelled at 26.6% of the field and appears in 1.2% of winning
lineups, Daniel Jones at 46.6% against 32.5%, Emari Demercado at 25.0% against
13.3%. Avoiding them costs nothing. There is no trade-off to manage here, and
a slate where the chalk is also correct would look very different.

THE $6,000 UNSPENT IN LINEUP 3 IS DELIBERATE and was chosen by the search, not
left by accident. Every way of spending it runs through Jonathan Taylor, Tyler
Warren or Daniel Jones, and each lowers the lineup's chance of clearing 180.

STANDING CAVEATS

Neither kicker is modelled. Fairbairn at $5,400 and Shrader at $5,200 are
invisible to this project. The field rostered kickers at 26% and 33% in the
two most recent showdowns and the 9 November winner carried two of them. Their
absence here is ignorance, not a fade.

Flex ownership carries about six points of mean error, roughly a quarter of a
typical value, and it cannot push low enough: the sub-10% group came in at
6.4% actual against 9.7% modelled. Read the field% column in bands. Do not
divide it into anything.

Field share is the SUM of six marginal shares. It is not the chance anybody
built the exact six. Entries are stacked rather than drawn independently, so
the slots are dependent, and duplicate counts need a model of how lineups get
constructed, which this is not.
