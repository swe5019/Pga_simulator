==============================================================================
WHY THESE THREE, AND WHAT WOULD MAKE THEM WRONG
==============================================================================

The shared structure. Every one of the five showdown winners scored so far was
CHALK IN FIVE SLOTS and different in exactly one: the captain. Their total
ownerships were 149.6%, 151.3%, 166.1%, 169.6% and 175.0%, and their duplicate
counts were 1, 8, 11, 13 and 32. Not one was a unique lineup. So these builds
do not try to be strange everywhere. C.J. Stroud is in all three flexes and
will be in most of the field's, which is the point: he is the best player in
the game and he costs the same whoever rosters him.

The quarterback is in the FLEX in all three. Five of five winners did that, and
none captained a quarterback. That record alone is thin, about a one in seven
coincidence if the simulation's own 33% rate is right, so the real reason is
mechanical: the captain slot multiplies salary as well as score, so it buys
concentration rather than value, and you want concentration on the widest right
tail. Stroud's 90th percentile is 1.59x his mean, the narrowest of any serious
option here, against 1.92x for Collins and 1.84x for Montgomery. A quarterback's
worth is his floor, and a floor wins nothing in a 643,587-entry tournament.

Jonathan Taylor is deliberately not a captain in any of these. He is the most
owned captain on the board at a modelled 20.2% and the simulation ranks him
fourth at a 6.5% win rate. He is also the most expensive captain at $15,900,
which strands the rest of the roster: the field's natural Taylor build tested
at a 163.7 ninety-ninth percentile, the lowest of anything considered here,
while carrying the highest captain ownership. Most owned and worst ceiling is
the trade that has punished the field in four of the five contests measured.
He is still in lineup 3's flex, where his price is fair.

WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS WRONG

Alec Pierce, $8,000, has no projection here because he has had ZERO prop lines
in every snapshot since 16 November, while Treadwell has had 18 to 22 in the
same window. He is not on the injury list either. The most likely reading is
that he is out and the book knows it, which is why Treadwell and Dulin carry
the Indianapolis receiving work in these builds. CONFIRM IT. If Pierce plays,
Treadwell and Dulin are badly overstated, lineup 3 is void, and lineups 1 and 2
need Treadwell replaced.

Neither kicker is modelled. Fairbairn at $5,400 and Shrader at $5,200 are
invisible to this project, and that is not a small gap in showdown: the field
rostered kickers at 26% and 33% in the two most recent contests, and the 9
November winner carried two of them. Their absence from these lineups is an
admission of ignorance, not a fade.

Captain ownership is modelled from salary alone, at roughly 3.4 points of mean
error out of sample. Read it as how crowded a choice is, not as a number to
divide a win rate by; that division ranked the actual winning captain 12th of
21 and 20th of 23 on the two contests where it could be checked.
