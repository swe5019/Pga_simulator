WHY THESE, FROM SEVEN SCORED SHOWDOWNS
==============================================================================

THE CHEAP PIECE IS NOT A SACRIFICE, IT IS THE WHOLE STRUCTURE. Forcing every
flex slot to be 20%-plus owned caps the best achievable lineup at a 4.22%
chance of clearing 151.8. Allowing one piece under 3% raises it to 15.94%.
Being hard to duplicate is not a cost here, it is four times better, because
the salary a 200-dollar tight end frees is what pays for Chase, Higgins and
both quarterbacks. Cam Grandy at 0.5% can be in at most 3,230 of 646,012
entries, and realistically in far fewer once the other five slots are fixed.

That is also what every low-duplicate winner has looked like. The 13 November
winner had ONE duplicate and carried Ben Sinnott at 0.6%. The 22 November
winner had four and carried Tai Felton at 7.8%. Chalk in the expensive slots,
one piece nobody has.

THE REST IS CHALK ON PURPOSE. All seven winners so far sat between 146.5% and
189.4% of summed ownership and NOT ONE was a unique lineup; duplicates ran
from 1 to 32. These three sit at 168.4%, 174.6% and 183.2%. A lineup nobody
else would build in six slots is not a differentiated lineup, it is a bad one.

THE CAPTAIN IS THE ONE REAL DECISION, so the three spread it rather than
spreading the roster. Leverage has gone to whoever captained the game's TOP
SCORER in all seven contests, between 3.4x and 11.2x, so the captain is chosen
on the chance of being the best player in the game and not on being cheap.
Burrow 26.9%, Daniels 15.3%, Higgins 11.3%.

WHAT COULD STILL BE WRONG

Kickers are now modelled but only to the point of being unbiased, and they
are uncorrelated with the rest of the pool, so their upside in a field-goal
game is understated. Neither kicker made these builds; that is a judgement,
not a measurement.

The flex ownership model carries about 7.5 points of mean error, roughly a
third of a typical value. Read every field% above as a band.

marginal ownership, not a forecast.
