What these NFL advanced stats mean

Box score numbers tell you what already happened. These tell you what is likely to happen next, because they measure opportunity rather than results. A receiver's catches depend on whether the ball arrived; his target share, snap share and air yards share depend only on the role his coaches gave him, and roles are far stickier week to week than touchdowns are.

Target share, snap share and WOPR

Target share is the percentage of his team's targets a player saw. Snap share is the percentage of his offense's snaps he was on the field for, which is what separates a genuine every-down player from a rotational one. Air yards share is his cut of the yardage his team threw toward, so it rewards the downfield role that target share alone misses. WOPR (weighted opportunity rating) folds the last two together at 1.5 times target share plus 0.7 times air yards share, and is the single best one-number summary of a receiver's workload.

Routes run, TPRR and yards per route run

Targets per route run is the cleanest usage rate in football: it asks how often a player was thrown to when he actually ran a route, so unlike target share it is not distorted by how often his team passed or how many snaps he rested. Yards per route run does the same for production. Routes here are reconstructed from which players were on the field for each dropback, because no one publishes charted route counts for free. Seasons without published participation data show the other columns instead of guessing.

Expected fantasy points and FPOE

Expected fantasy points price every target and carry by its depth and field position, so they describe what a player's usage was worth regardless of whether he converted it. FPOE is his actual half PPR points per game minus that figure. A negative FPOE is usually the buy signal: the role is already there and the scoring has not caught up. A large positive one tends to regress, because it was built on touchdowns the volume will not keep producing. For fantasy football that makes it a waiver and buy-low tool; for DFS it finds the players the market has not repriced yet.

Where the data comes from

Everything is built from nflverse play by play, snap counts and NFL Next Gen Stats, with expected points from ffopportunity and DraftKings salaries for the current slate. It refreshes every Tuesday once Monday night is graded, and every figure is automatically recomputed from the sources and compared against the published file before it goes live. Rates are recalculated over whichever week range you select rather than averaged across weeks, so a role that changed mid-season shows up cleanly instead of being blurred away.

What is a good target share in the NFL?

Roughly 20% marks a clear number one option, 25% and up is a genuine alpha receiver, and anything past 30% is among the most concentrated roles in the league. Below about 15% a player usually needs a touchdown to pay off.

What is FPOE in fantasy football?

FPOE is fantasy points over expected: actual half PPR points per game minus what the player's opportunity was worth. Negative means his usage has not yet turned into scoring, which often makes him a buy. Positive usually means touchdown luck that will not repeat.
